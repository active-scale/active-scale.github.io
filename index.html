Why do robots need active perception?

A fixed camera sees one slice of the scene. When the target is inside a bag, under a table, or somewhere on a shelf, the robot has to move its view before it can act, and keep track of what it saw along the way.

ViA’s FAQ ↗ explains why a wider field of view cannot remove occlusions, and why moving the camera helps reveal hidden objects.

How do we design and teleoperate AMP?

A policy learns to coordinate its view with its hands only from demonstrations where both are coordinated. AMP, our Active-perception Mobile-manipulation Platform, adds an independent camera arm to a bimanual mobile robot, and one operator drives the camera, both arms and the base.

One mobile base. Three arms.

We keep the Cobot Magic’s mobile base, lower support and two PiPER arms, and replace its fixed front camera with a third PiPER arm on a 100 × 100 × 300 mm aluminum extrusion. The viewpoint now moves independently of both hands.

How does one operator control AMP?

The headset steers the camera arm, the two controllers steer the arms and grippers, and controller buttons drive the base, so one person can look, reach and move at once.

How can in-the-wild data teach robots to look?

Human egocentric video shows where people look, but not how a robot should move. We learn viewpoint and manipulation priors from human data while keeping robot data in the mix, so the actions stay grounded.

How does the model learn to look?

Seeing more frames is not enough; the policy has to know where each frame was taken. Every front-camera frame gets a camera token trained to predict its pose, so the model can relate past views to the current one before it acts.

Can it solve active-perception tasks?

Five real-world tasks spanning occlusion-driven manipulation and active search and localization.

Does the extra design slow down inference?

Each new observation conditions the next action chunk while the robot executes the current one. Real-time chunking (RTC) keeps end-effector pose control continuous at 30 Hz. ActiveScale generates 264.5 actions/s on one RTX 4090.

Conclusion and limitations

A recipe for looking and acting.

ActiveScale combines camera-aware temporal modeling, human–robot mid-training, and AMP’s independent viewpoint control. In our ablations, adding observation history alone helped some tasks and hurt others; adding camera tokens on top improved all five.

Across five real-world task families, mean success improves from 30% to 70% compared with directly post-trained π₀.₅.

What comes next?

Our evaluation covers five task families. The mobile demonstration shows single-operator teleoperation; broader autonomous mobile active-perception evaluation remains future work.

This study uses mid-training to learn from egocentric data. Next steps include larger and more diverse active-perception pretraining datasets and scaled AMP data collection.
